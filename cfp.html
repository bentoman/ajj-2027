AJJ 2027 · Call for proposals

Shifting Borders

12–13 June 2027 · Nanzan University, Nagoya, Japan

Draft for review

The CFP and submission form are being finalized. Submissions are not open yet. Planned circulation: 1 December 2026.

The 2027 conference of Anthropology of Japan in Japan (AJJ) invites proposals for individual presentations, themed panels, workshops, roundtables, and other formats exploring “Shifting Borders.”

Borders are often imagined as fixed lines separating nations, communities, disciplines, or identities. Yet anthropology has long demonstrated that boundaries are continually negotiated, challenged, crossed, and redefined. How do borders take shape in everyday life? Who has the power to draw or contest them? What happens when boundaries shift—and when they remain stubbornly in place?

In contemporary Japan and beyond, migration, mobility, demographic change, technological transformation, and environmental challenges are reshaping how people understand themselves and their relationships with others. Evolving forms of belonging bring new possibilities for connection alongside persistent experiences of exclusion. Anthropologists are also reconsidering boundaries between the local and the global, the human and non-human, the physical and digital, and the public and private.

We welcome ethnographic, theoretical, methodological, and interdisciplinary contributions examining how borders are created, maintained, experienced, contested, or transformed. Proposals may address Japan, connections between Japan and other places, or comparative perspectives that contribute to discussions of Japan.

Possible topics

  • Migration, mobility, citizenship, and belonging
  • Identity, language, and the negotiation of difference
  • Disability, access, care, and institutional boundaries
  • Religion, ritual, heritage, and cultural transmission
  • Demographic change, rural life, and urban transformation
  • Media, technology, and physical and digital spaces
  • Environment, disaster, and human–non-human relationships
  • Health, education, work, and everyday inclusion and exclusion
  • Disciplinary boundaries, collaboration, ethics, and representation

These topics are indicative rather than exhaustive. We welcome unexpected interpretations of the theme and contributions to the broader anthropological study of Japan, from researchers at all career stages, postgraduate students, independent scholars, and practitioners. Completed research and work in progress are welcome.

Proposal formats and requirements

Individual presentations

Submit a title and abstract of up to 250 words. List all authors and affiliations, indicate who will present, and include three to five keywords. The organizers will group accepted presentations into sessions.

Themed panels

Submit one proposal for the complete panel: a title, overview of up to 300 words, and a title and abstract of up to 250 words for each presentation. Identify the organizer, chair, and respondent (discussant), with names, affiliations, and roles.

A standard panel has three presenters and a respondent in a 90-minute session. Suggested allocation: 20 minutes per presentation, 10–15 minutes for the respondent, and 10–15 minutes for audience Q&A, with remaining time for introductions and transitions. Alternative structures may be proposed; final arrangements will be agreed with the organizers.

Workshops, roundtables, and other formats

Submit a title and proposal of up to 300 words describing the focus, connection to the theme, and activities or discussion questions. Identify contributors and roles, proposed duration, audience participation, and any room or equipment requirements. Film, visual anthropology, and collaborative formats are welcome.

For all proposals

Include the contact person’s name, email, and affiliation; brief biographies of up to 100 words per contributor; three to five keywords; the proposed presentation language; and any practical requirements. Access arrangements may be discussed privately with the organizers; no medical information is needed.

Dates and submission

  • Submission deadline: 31 January 2027, 23:59 Japan Standard Time
  • Notification of decisions: by 28 February 2027
  • Preliminary programme: late March 2027

Proposals will be reviewed by Benjamin Dorman, Andreas Riessland, and Robert Croker.
