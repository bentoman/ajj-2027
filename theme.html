Conference theme

Shifting Borders

Anthropological Perspectives on Change in Japan and Beyond

The 2027 conference of Anthropology of Japan in Japan (AJJ) invites participants to explore the theme of “Shifting Borders.” Borders are often imagined as fixed lines separating nations, communities, disciplines, or identities. Yet anthropology has long demonstrated that boundaries are continually negotiated, challenged, crossed, and redefined.

In contemporary Japan and beyond, shifting borders can be observed in many domains of social and cultural life. Migration, mobility, demographic change, technological transformation, environmental challenges, and evolving forms of belonging continue to reshape how people understand themselves and their relationships with others. At the same time, anthropologists are increasingly examining boundaries between the local and the global, the human and non-human, the physical and digital, and the public and private.

This conference welcomes papers that examine how borders are created, maintained, contested, or transformed. We encourage contributions from all areas of anthropology and related fields, including studies of identity, migration, heritage, religion, language, media, politics, environment, health, education, and everyday life.

By bringing together diverse perspectives, AJJ 2027 seeks to explore how changing boundaries shape contemporary experiences and how anthropological approaches can illuminate emerging forms of connection, difference, and belonging.
